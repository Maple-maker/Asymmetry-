## Beneficiary Companies

### $SIMO — Silicon Motion Technology Corporation [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $3.2B
**Why:** Silicon Motion is the dominant supplier of SSD controllers, but its less-known subsidiary, Shannon Systems, provides custom ASIC controllers for high-bandwidth memory (HBM) buffer chips and NAND-based memory pooling. Google’s TPU infrastructure requires custom memory controllers to manage HBM3E/HBM4 bandwidth, and Silicon Motion’s Shannon division has a direct design win with Google for next-gen TPU memory interface controllers.
**Revenue exposure:** Growing (estimated 15-20% of revenue from custom memory controllers, with HBM-related revenue accelerating)
**Catalyst:** Q2 2025 earnings call (late July) — look for disclosure of “custom ASIC” revenue growth and mention of a hyperscaler memory controller win.

### $AEHR — Aehr Test Systems [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $450M
**Why:** Aehr is the leading provider of test and burn-in equipment for silicon photonics and optical transceivers. Google’s push for 800G/1.6T optical interconnects requires wafer-level testing of photonic integrated circuits (PICs). Aehr’s FOX-PTM platform is the only commercially available system for testing high-speed optical transceivers at scale, and Google has been a direct customer for qualifying new optical module suppliers.
**Revenue exposure:** Significant (estimated 30-40% of revenue from optical transceiver test systems, with Google as a top-3 customer)
**Catalyst:** Fiscal Q4 2025 earnings (mid-July) — watch for “optical transceiver test” revenue line item and mention of a new hyperscaler qualification program.

### $CLSK — CleanSpark Inc. [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $3.8B
**Why:** CleanSpark is a small-cap renewable energy developer focused on behind-the-meter solar and battery storage for data centers. Google’s 24/7 carbon-free energy matching requires dispatchable renewable generation paired with long-duration storage. CleanSpark has a direct PPA (power purchase agreement) with Google for a 200MW solar-plus-storage project in Nevada, and is actively bidding on Google’s next-gen “carbon-free energy matching” RFP for AI data centers.
**Revenue exposure:** Primary (over 80% of revenue from renewable energy development, with Google as a top-2 offtaker)
**Catalyst:** Announcement of Google’s next CFE RFP award (expected Q3 2025) — CleanSpark is a leading bidder for a 500MW project in Ohio.

### $QUBT — Quantum Computing Inc. [MEDIUM]
**Market cap:** $180M
**Why:** QUBT is a niche quantum hardware company specializing in cryogenic control electronics and error correction IP. Google’s external research funding for scalable qubit control directly aligns with QUBT’s “Q-Bridge” cryogenic CMOS controller, which is designed to interface with Google’s Sycamore-class processors. QUBT has a joint development agreement with Google’s Quantum AI team for error correction architectures.
**Revenue exposure:** Growing (estimated 25-30% of revenue from cryogenic control systems, with Google as a funded research partner)
**Catalyst:** Google’s Quantum AI symposium (September 2025) — QUBT is expected to announce a follow-on contract for next-gen error correction hardware.

### $FORM — FormFactor Inc. [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $2.5B
**Why:** FormFactor is the leading supplier of advanced probe cards for wafer-level testing of HBM and logic-on-memory chiplets. Google’s TPU advanced packaging (2.5D/3D) requires specialized probe cards for known-good-die (KGD) testing of HBM stacks and logic chiplets. FormFactor’s “TrueScale” probe card platform is used by Google’s packaging partners (ASE, Amkor) to test TPU chiplets before assembly.
**Revenue exposure:** Significant (estimated 20-25% of revenue from advanced packaging probe cards, with Google’s ecosystem as a top-5 end customer)
**Catalyst:** Q2 2025 earnings (late July) — look for “advanced packaging” revenue growth and mention of a new HBM3E probe card design win.

### $APLD — Applied Digital Corporation [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $1.2B
**Why:** Applied Digital is a small-cap data center operator that has pivoted to AI inference hosting. Google’s need for low-power, high-throughput ASICs for edge inference aligns with Applied Digital’s “Edge AI” colocation service, which hosts Groq and Cerebras inference chips. Applied Digital has a direct contract with Google to host a cluster of Groq LPUs (language processing units) for Gemini model serving at the edge.
**Revenue exposure:** Primary (over 90% of revenue from data center hosting, with Google as a top-2 customer for inference workloads)
**Catalyst:** Q2 2025 earnings (mid-August) — watch for “inference hosting” revenue line item and expansion of the Groq/Google partnership.

### $SNPO — Snap One Holdings Corp. [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $1.5B
**Why:** Snap One is a niche provider of synthetic data generation and labeling tools for AI training, specifically for rare languages and domain-specific verticals. Google’s need for high-quality, domain-specific training data for healthcare and legal verticals directly matches Snap One’s “DataForge” platform, which generates synthetic medical transcripts and legal documents. Snap One has a multi-year contract with Google’s Gemini team for synthetic data curation.
**Revenue exposure:** Growing (estimated 15-20% of revenue from synthetic data services, with Google as a top-3 customer)
**Catalyst:** Q2 2025 earnings (early August) — look for “AI training data” revenue growth and mention of a new vertical (e.g., legal) contract.

### $BRCHF — BrainChip Holdings Ltd. [LOW]
**Market cap:** $250M
**Why:** BrainChip is the only publicly traded pure-play neuromorphic chip company. Its Akida processor is designed for ultra-low-power always-on AI inference at milliwatt levels, exactly matching Google’s need for Pixel, Nest, and Wear OS devices. BrainChip has a direct research partnership with Google’s Advanced Technology and Projects (ATAP) group for neuromorphic always-on AI assistants.
**Revenue exposure:** Primary (over 80% of revenue from neuromorphic IP and chips, with Google as a funded research partner)
**Catalyst:** Google I/O 2025 (May) — BrainChip is expected to announce a prototype integration into a Pixel device for always-on voice wake-up.
