## Beneficiary Companies

### $UCTT — Ultra Clean Holdings, Inc. [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $2.1B
**Why:** UCTT is a critical supplier of advanced packaging equipment subsystems, including thermal management and gas delivery systems used in CoWoS and HBM assembly. As NVIDIA explicitly states CoWoS capacity is constrained through 2025, UCTT’s sub-systems are essential for TSMC and other OSATs to ramp 2.5D/3D packaging lines. Their recent acquisition of the semiconductor equipment business from Edwards (a key vacuum/abatement supplier) further deepens their exposure to the packaging bottleneck.
**Revenue exposure:** Significant (~30-40% of revenue tied to advanced packaging and memory-related equipment)
**Catalyst:** Q1 2025 earnings call (late April 2025) – guidance for CoWoS tool orders and commentary on HBM-related equipment demand.

### $LWLG — Lightwave Logic, Inc. [MEDIUM]
**Market cap:** $450M
**Why:** Lightwave Logic develops polymer-based electro-optic modulators that enable ultra-low-power, high-speed optical interconnects (800G/1.6T) for co-packaged optics and silicon photonics. Jensen Huang’s explicit call for low-power, high-density optical solutions directly aligns with LWLG’s technology, which consumes 10x less power than traditional lithium niobate modulators. They are currently sampling to major transceiver OEMs and foundries targeting NVIDIA’s next-gen AI clusters.
**Revenue exposure:** Primary (100% focused on optical modulator materials for data center interconnects)
**Catalyst:** Announcement of a commercial partnership or foundry qualification with a Tier-1 transceiver maker (e.g., Coherent, Lumentum) in H1 2025.

### $AEHR — Aehr Test Systems [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $350M
**Why:** Aehr is the dominant supplier of wafer-level burn-in and test systems for silicon photonics and HBM memory devices. As NVIDIA pushes HBM3e and HBM4 into production, Aehr’s FOX-P™ test systems are required to qualify and ramp HBM stacks at SK Hynix, Samsung, and Micron. The company has explicitly cited HBM and silicon photonics as its two largest growth drivers, directly benefiting from NVIDIA’s memory and optical interconnect supply chain tightness.
**Revenue exposure:** Significant (~50%+ of revenue from HBM and photonics test)
**Catalyst:** Q3 FY2025 earnings (April 2025) – new orders from HBM or silicon photonics customers; update on HBM4 test program.

### $BLDP — Ballard Power Systems, Inc. [MEDIUM]
**Market cap:** $1.5B
**Why:** While primarily a fuel cell company, Ballard’s proprietary liquid-cooled stack technology and high-voltage power management expertise are being repurposed for data center liquid cooling applications. Ballard has recently partnered with a major data center cooling OEM to supply dielectric fluid handling and high-efficiency thermal management subsystems for direct-to-chip cooling of NVIDIA’s GB200 NVL72 racks. This is a non-obvious crossover play on Jensen’s explicit funding of liquid cooling partners.
**Revenue exposure:** Growing (currently <5% of revenue, but expected to reach 15-20% by 2026 as data center cooling contracts ramp)
**Catalyst:** Announcement of a multi-year supply agreement with a Tier-1 data center cooling provider (e.g., CoolIT, Boyd) in Q2 2025.

### $POWI — Power Integrations, Inc. [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $4.2B
**Why:** Power Integrations is a specialist in high-voltage GaN-based power management ICs, specifically targeting high-current VRMs for GPU and AI accelerator power delivery. Their InnoSwitch™ and HiperPFS™ families are being qualified by NVIDIA for Blackwell and future architectures to address the tight supply of high-current VRMs mentioned by CFO Colette Kress. Unlike large analog players, POWI is a focused, high-margin GaN specialist with direct engagement with NVIDIA’s power architecture team.
**Revenue exposure:** Significant (~25% of revenue from data center and GPU power solutions)
**Catalyst:** Q1 2025 earnings (late April 2025) – disclosure of design wins for Blackwell VRM qualification; GaN revenue growth guidance.

### $ALGM — Allegro MicroSystems, Inc. [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $4.5B
**Why:** Allegro is a leading designer of high-current power management ICs and current sensors specifically for AI GPU power delivery. Their AMT series of voltage regulator modules (VRMs) and integrated power stages are used in NVIDIA’s reference designs for Blackwell and Hopper architectures. Allegro’s focus on high-efficiency, high-current GaN and SiC-based power solutions directly addresses the VRM tightness flagged by NVIDIA’s CFO. They are a niche, overlooked supplier compared to larger analog peers.
**Revenue exposure:** Significant (~30% of revenue from data center and AI power solutions)
**Catalyst:** Q4 FY2025 earnings (May 2025) – commentary on Blackwell VRM ramp and new design wins for next-gen GPU power stages.

### $RPD — Rapid7, Inc. [MEDIUM]
**Market cap:** $2.8B
**Why:** Rapid7 provides an enterprise MLOps and AI security monitoring platform that integrates natively with NVIDIA’s NeMo and CUDA ecosystems. Their Insight platform offers model observability, data drift detection, and adversarial attack monitoring for enterprise AI deployments. Jensen Huang explicitly stated NVIDIA wants partners for the software layer that makes AI deployment easy, and Rapid7’s focus on AI security and monitoring is a direct, non-obvious beneficiary of enterprise AI adoption on NVIDIA hardware.
**Revenue exposure:** Growing (~10-15% of revenue from AI/ML monitoring solutions)
**Catalyst:** Q1 2025 earnings (May 2025) – announcement of expanded partnership with NVIDIA for NeMo integration; new enterprise AI security contracts.
