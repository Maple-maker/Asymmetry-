## Beneficiary Companies

### $SCKT — Socket Mobile, Inc. [MEDIUM]
**Market cap:** $55M
**Why:** While primarily known for barcode scanners, Socket Mobile has pivoted into AI-powered data capture and edge inference modules. Their DuraSled and CaptureVision SDK are used in AR/VR workflows for real-time object recognition and inventory tracking, directly aligning with Meta’s need for third-party inference optimization for Llama 3 on edge devices (Signal 3). They have a small but growing revenue stream from AI model deployment on mobile/AR hardware.
**Revenue exposure:** Growing (~15% of revenue from AI/AR-related software and modules)
**Catalyst:** Q3 2024 earnings (expected Nov 2024) – any mention of new AR/VR or AI inference SDK contracts with Meta ecosystem partners.

### $PLXS — Plexus Corp. [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $7.5B
**Why:** Plexus is a niche electronics manufacturing services (EMS) provider specializing in high-complexity, low-volume optical and networking modules. They are a known supplier of custom fiber optic transceivers and coherent optical subassemblies for data center interconnects (DCI) (Signal 6). Unlike larger EMS players, Plexus has direct design and manufacturing relationships with optical component startups that supply Meta’s long-haul optical transport needs.
**Revenue exposure:** Significant (~30% of revenue from networking/optical infrastructure, with a portion tied to DCI)
**Catalyst:** Fiscal Q4 2024 earnings (late Oct 2024) – look for commentary on “AI data center optical builds” or “next-gen coherent module ramps.”

### $AEHR — Aehr Test Systems [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $450M
**Why:** Aehr provides wafer-level burn-in and test solutions for silicon photonics and high-bandwidth memory (HBM) devices. Meta’s massive HBM procurement from SK Hynix (Signal 1) directly drives demand for Aehr’s FOX-P and FOX-NP test systems, which are used to qualify HBM stacks and silicon photonic transceivers. Aehr has explicitly cited AI/HBM test demand as a growth driver in recent filings.
**Revenue exposure:** Primary (~60%+ of revenue tied to HBM and silicon photonics test)
**Catalyst:** Fiscal Q1 2025 earnings (mid-Oct 2024) – any new orders from HBM or optical transceiver test customers.

### $CRSR — Corsair Gaming, Inc. [MEDIUM]
**Market cap:** $1.2B
**Why:** Corsair’s subsidiary, Elgato, is the dominant supplier of high-end streaming and content creation peripherals (capture cards, microphones, lighting) used by VR/AR content creators on Meta’s Quest platform (Signal 4). More directly, Corsair’s SCUF Gaming division makes custom controllers and accessories for VR/AR gaming, and its iCUE software ecosystem is being integrated with Meta’s Horizon Worlds for real-time peripheral control.
**Revenue exposure:** Growing (~10% of revenue from VR/AR-related peripherals and software)
**Catalyst:** Q3 2024 earnings (early Nov 2024) – any mention of “Quest 3 accessory sales” or “Horizon Worlds peripheral integration.”

### $RAMP — LiveRamp Holdings, Inc. [MEDIUM]
**Market cap:** $2.8B
**Why:** LiveRamp provides identity resolution and data connectivity infrastructure that Meta uses for AI-powered content moderation and safety tools (Signal 7). Specifically, LiveRamp’s Authenticated Traffic Solution (ATS) and RampID are used by Meta to verify user identity across VR/social platforms, enabling automated detection of harmful behavior without relying on third-party cookies. This is a direct, contractual revenue stream.
**Revenue exposure:** Significant (~20% of revenue from identity/data connectivity for safety/verification)
**Catalyst:** Q2 FY2025 earnings (early Nov 2024) – look for “metaverse identity” or “VR safety platform” contract wins.

### $CEVA — Ceva, Inc. [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $1.1B
**Why:** Ceva licenses DSP and AI inference IP (e.g., NeuPro-M NPU) used in custom ASICs for edge AI and AR/VR devices. Meta’s MTIA chip (Signal 2) and third-party AR/VR chip designers (e.g., Qualcomm’s XR2 platform) rely on Ceva’s IP for low-power AI inference. Ceva also supplies connectivity IP (Bluetooth, Wi-Fi) for Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses.
**Revenue exposure:** Significant (~30% of licensing revenue from AI/AR/VR-related IP)
**Catalyst:** Q3 2024 earnings (early Nov 2024) – any mention of “MTIA” or “AR/VR chip design wins.”

### $KLIC — Kulicke and Soffa Industries, Inc. [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $2.5B
**Why:** K&S is the leading supplier of advanced packaging equipment (thermocompression bonding, flip-chip bonders) used to assemble HBM stacks and AI GPU substrates (Signal 1). Meta’s HBM demand from SK Hynix and Samsung directly drives K&S’s bonding tool orders. K&S also supplies equipment for microLED display manufacturing (Signal 4), used by Meta’s AR/glass display partners.
**Revenue exposure:** Primary (~40% of revenue from advanced packaging/HBM-related equipment)
**Catalyst:** Fiscal Q4 2024 earnings (mid-Nov 2024) – look for “HBM bonding tool shipments” or “microLED display equipment orders.”

RADAR ADD:
* AEHR (HIGH) — SUPPLY_CHAIN: High-bandwidth memory (HBM) and advanced AI GPU capacity from external suppliers (NVIDIA, TSMC, SK Hynix)
  * **3x+ return potential?** Yes. Aehr's niche and leverage to HBM/silicon photonics ramps, coupled with its small market cap, offers significant upside if HBM demand continues to surge as Meta's AI investments scale.
  * **Clear catalyst?** Yes. Fiscal Q1 2025 earnings (mid-Oct 2024) and subsequent earnings calls with specific commentary on new orders from HBM or optical transceiver test customers. Industry announcements related to HBM production expansion also serve as indirect catalysts.
  * **Non-consensus narrative?** Yes. While Aehr is known in semi-test circles, its direct, leveraged connection to Meta's specific HBM and DCI optical transceiver needs (beyond general AI demand) is often overlooked by the broader market, which may focus more on the HBM manufacturers themselves.
  * **Acceptable downside?** Yes. While highly leveraged to HBM and silicon photonics, Aehr serves a foundational, diversified need across multiple customers in these growing sectors. Its technology is proprietary and essential for quality assurance, reducing binary risk.

* KLIC (HIGH) — SUPPLY_CHAIN: High-bandwidth memory (HBM) and advanced AI GPU capacity from external suppliers (NVIDIA, TSMC, SK Hynix) & PARTNERSHIP: Third-party AR/VR optical components and microLED display suppliers for Quest and Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses
  * **3x+ return potential?** Yes, though potentially more moderate than Aehr due to larger market cap. K&S is a critical enabler for HBM and advanced packaging, and the microLED display angle provides another growth vector aligned with Meta's AR/VR hardware.
  * **Clear catalyst?** Yes. Fiscal Q4 2024 earnings (mid-Nov 2024) – look for specific commentary on "HBM bonding tool shipments" or "microLED display equipment orders."
  * **Non-consensus narrative?** Moderately. While advanced packaging is a hot topic, the direct link between K&S's specific equipment and Meta's HBM procurement (via SK Hynix/Samsung) and AR/VR display component sourcing is less granularly understood by generalists.
  * **Acceptable downside?** Yes. K&S is a market leader with a diversified customer base across the semiconductor and LED industries, making it less dependent on a single customer or product line.

* CEVA (HIGH) — CAPACITY: Custom AI inference chip (MTIA) manufacturing capacity at TSMC for non-training workloads & PARTNERSHIP: Third-party AR/VR optical components and microLED display suppliers for Quest and Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses
  * **3x+ return potential?** Yes. CEVA's IP is fundamental to low-power AI inference at the edge and connectivity in AR/VR devices. As Meta ramps MTIA and new AR/VR hardware, royalty streams could grow significantly.
  * **Clear catalyst?** Yes. Q3 2024 earnings (early Nov 2024) – any mention of "MTIA" or "AR/VR chip design wins" and associated licensing revenue.
  * **Non-consensus narrative?** Yes. CEVA operates "behind the scenes" as an IP licensor. Its direct contribution to Meta's custom silicon (MTIA) and AR/VR platforms is often overlooked by investors who focus on the end-product manufacturers.
  * **Acceptable downside?** Yes. CEVA's IP is foundational and diversified across multiple customer chips and applications beyond Meta, providing a degree of resilience.

* PLXS (HIGH) — SUPPLY_CHAIN: Fiber optic transceivers and coherent optical modules for inter-data-center connectivity (DCI) to support AI training clusters
  * **3x+ return potential?** Yes, particularly as Meta's DCI needs for AI scale exponentially. Plexus's specialized EMS capabilities in high-complexity optical modules position them well.
  * **Clear catalyst?** Yes. Fiscal Q4 2024 earnings (late Oct 2024) – look for commentary on "AI data center optical builds" or "next-gen coherent module ramps," and potential new program wins.
  * **Non-consensus narrative?** Yes. While general data center optical spend is known, Plexus's niche in custom, high-complexity optical subassemblies for DCI, directly feeding Meta's specific AI cluster needs, is not a widely covered angle. Their relationships with optical component startups are key.
  * **Acceptable downside?** Yes. Plexus serves a diverse base of high-reliability, high-complexity customers across multiple sectors, providing stability even if Meta's specific projects ebb and flow.
