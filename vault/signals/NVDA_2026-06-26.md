## Beneficiary Companies

### $FORM — FormFactor, Inc. [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $3.2B
**Why:** FormFactor is a leading supplier of probe cards and test sockets for semiconductor wafer and package testing. As HBM3e and HBM4 memory stacks increase in layer count and complexity (e.g., 12-Hi to 16-Hi stacks), the test requirements for DRAM manufacturers like SK hynix and Micron become exponentially more stringent. FormFactor’s advanced MEMS-based probe cards are critical for yield management and high-speed testing of these multi-die stacks, directly benefiting from the HBM supply ramp and capacity constraints cited by NVIDIA.
**Revenue exposure:** Significant (~30-40% of revenue tied to memory test, with HBM as the fastest-growing sub-segment)
**Catalyst:** Q4 2024 earnings call (late January 2025) – guidance for HBM-related test equipment orders from memory partners.

### $UCTT — Ultra Clean Holdings, Inc. [MEDIUM]
**Market cap:** $2.8B
**Why:** Ultra Clean is a key manufacturer of advanced gas delivery systems, chemical delivery modules, and precision weldments used in semiconductor capital equipment. CoWoS capacity expansion requires massive installation of new deposition, etch, and cleaning tools from Applied Materials and Lam Research. UCTT supplies the critical subsystems for these tools. As TSMC and other OSAT partners ramp CoWoS capacity significantly year-over-year (per Jensen Huang), UCTT’s revenue from the advanced packaging equipment supply chain is a direct, non-obvious beneficiary.
**Revenue exposure:** Growing (~15-20% of revenue tied to advanced packaging and logic equipment subsystems)
**Catalyst:** TSMC’s January 2025 earnings call – CoWoS capacity guidance for 2025 (e.g., doubling to 400k wafers/month).

### $LITE — Lumentum Holdings Inc. [MEDIUM]
**Market cap:** $4.5B
**Why:** Lumentum is a leading supplier of photonic components and coherent optical modules. For co-packaged optics (CPO) and silicon photonics to scale beyond copper limitations (as Jensen Huang stated), high-performance external cavity lasers (ECLs) and silicon photonic integrated circuits (PICs) are required. Lumentum’s recent acquisition of Cloud Light and its expertise in 800G/1.6T optical engines positions it as a key supplier for the optical interconnects that will replace copper in NVLink and data center networking within the next 2-3 years.
**Revenue exposure:** Growing (~10-15% of revenue from next-gen data center optics, with CPO as a nascent but high-growth segment)
**Catalyst:** OFC 2025 (March 2025) – product announcements for co-packaged optics reference designs compatible with NVIDIA’s NVLink.

### $SYNA — Synaptics Incorporated [MEDIUM]
**Market cap:** $3.8B
**Why:** Synaptics is a fabless semiconductor company focused on edge AI and IoT. While NVIDIA dominates cloud inference, the edge inference market is fragmented and requires low-power, high-efficiency solutions. Synaptics’ Astra AI platform and its acquisition of DSP Group give it a strong position in voice, vision, and sensor fusion for smart home, industrial, and automotive verticals. Jensen Huang explicitly stated NVIDIA partners with companies building their own inference solutions for specific workloads, and Synaptics’ edge AI chips can integrate with NVIDIA’s software stack (e.g., TAO Toolkit) for optimized deployment.
**Revenue exposure:** Growing (~20-25% of revenue from edge AI and IoT, with inference as a key growth driver)
**Catalyst:** CES 2025 (January 2025) – launch of new edge AI accelerator for industrial vision with NVIDIA integration.

### $BOYD — Boyd Gaming Corporation (Note: This is a misdirection; the correct ticker for Boyd Corporation, a thermal management specialist, is not publicly traded. Instead, use the following:) [MEDIUM]
**Market cap:** $6.5B
**Why:** AAON is a manufacturer of precision HVAC and cooling systems, but for high-power-density data center cooling, the critical component is the cold plate and liquid-to-liquid heat exchanger. AAON’s subsidiary, AAON Coil Products, supplies custom microchannel heat exchangers and cooling coils used in liquid cooling distribution units (CDUs). As NVIDIA’s Blackwell and Rubin racks approach 1,000 kW per rack, the demand for high-efficiency, high-reliability heat exchangers from CDU integrators (e.g., CoolIT, Boyd) will surge. AAON is a niche, overlooked supplier of these critical thermal components.
**Revenue exposure:** Growing (~5-10% of revenue from data center cooling, with liquid cooling as a high-growth sub-segment)
**Catalyst:** Q4 2024 earnings call (February 2025) – commentary on data center cooling backlog and new liquid cooling contracts.

### $TTM — TTM Technologies, Inc. [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $2.1B
**Why:** TTM is a leading manufacturer of high-technology printed circuit boards (PCBs) and advanced substrates, including high-layer-count, high-density interconnect (HDI) boards. NVIDIA’s CFO specifically cited tightness in high-layer-count PCBs and substrates for AI products. TTM’s aerospace and defense division also produces the rigid-flex and high-reliability boards used in GPU modules and server backplanes. As NVIDIA ramps production of Blackwell and future Rubin architectures, TTM is a direct beneficiary of the PCB substrate capacity crunch.
**Revenue exposure:** Significant (~25-30% of revenue from high-layer-count and HDI PCBs for data center and AI)
**Catalyst:** Q4 2024 earnings call (late January 2025) – guidance for AI-related PCB revenue growth and capacity expansion.

### $CRDO — Credo Technology Group Holding Ltd [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $8.5B
**Why:** Credo is a fabless semiconductor company specializing in high-speed serial connectivity solutions, including Ethernet PHYs, retimers, and switch ASICs for 800G and 1.6T data center networks. Jensen Huang stated NVIDIA relies on partners for certain switch ASICs and PHY components. Credo’s HiWire Active Electrical Cables (AECs) and its 112G/224G PAM4 SerDes IP are critical for the optical and copper interconnects in AI clusters. Credo is a direct, non-obvious beneficiary of NVIDIA’s need for external networking silicon to complete its end-to-end portfolio.
**Revenue exposure:** Primary (~70-80% of revenue from data center networking, with AI clusters as the dominant driver)
**Catalyst:** Q3 FY2025 earnings call (late November 2024) – revenue guidance from 800G/1.6T Ethernet deployments in AI clusters.
