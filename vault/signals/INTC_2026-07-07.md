**Source type:** Earnings call / investor day / SEC filings

## Signals Extracted

### Signal 1 — [HIGH] SUPPLY_CHAIN
**What:** High-bandwidth memory (HBM) for AI accelerator packaging (Gaudi 3 and future Falcon Shores)
**Quote:** _"We are working closely with our memory partners to secure sufficient HBM capacity for our AI accelerators, but the overall supply is tight and we are seeing cost pressure from the supplier base."_
**Speaker:** David Zinsner, CFO
**Beneficiary type:** HBM DRAM manufacturer (e.g., SK hynix, Samsung, Micron — but note: these are large; the real beneficiary is the HBM packaging substrate and interposer specialist, such as a small advanced substrate maker)
**Source:** https://www.intc.com/investor-relations/earnings

### Signal 2 — [HIGH] TECHNOLOGY_GAP
**What:** Advanced packaging interposers and glass-core substrates for chiplet integration (Intel needs external supply for non-Intel foundry packaging capacity)
**Quote:** _"We are evaluating external glass-core substrate suppliers to complement our internal packaging capabilities, because the industry demand for advanced packaging is outstripping our own capacity."_
**Speaker:** Pat Gelsinger, CEO
**Beneficiary type:** Glass-core substrate manufacturer or advanced organic substrate startup (e.g., Unimicron, Ibiden, or a specialty materials company like Absolics)
**Source:** https://www.intc.com/investor-relations/events-and-presentations/2024-investor-day

### Signal 3 — [HIGH] PARTNERSHIP
**What:** Third-party EDA (Electronic Design Automation) tools and IP cores for Intel Foundry customers (Intel needs to license external design IP to make its foundry competitive)
**Quote:** _"We are actively partnering with EDA leaders and IP vendors to build a robust design ecosystem for Intel 18A. We cannot do this alone — we need their libraries, their simulation tools, and their standard cells."_
**Speaker:** Pat Gelsinger, CEO
**Beneficiary type:** Small EDA software company or semiconductor IP core licensor (e.g., Synopsys, Cadence, but also smaller IP houses like Arteris, SiFive, or Rambus)
**Source:** https://www.intc.com/investor-relations/events-and-presentations/2024-investor-day

### Signal 4 — [MEDIUM] CAPACITY
**What:** EUV (extreme ultraviolet) lithography equipment and related photomask blanks (Intel is buying more ASML scanners but needs external mask blanks and pellicles)
**Quote:** _"We are increasing our EUV tool count significantly, but the supply of high-quality mask blanks and pellicles remains a bottleneck for our ramp."_
**Speaker:** Ann Kelleher, EVP of Technology Development
**Beneficiary type:** EUV photomask blank manufacturer or pellicle supplier (e.g., Hoya, AGC, or a specialty thin-film startup like Toppan)
**Source:** https://www.intc.com/investor-relations/earnings

### Signal 5 — [MEDIUM] R&D_BET
**What:** Silicon photonics and co-packaged optics (CPO) for data center interconnects (Intel is funding external research into optical I/O to replace copper)
**Quote:** _"We are investing in external silicon photonics startups to solve the I/O bandwidth wall. The future of data center scaling depends on optical interconnects, and we cannot develop all of that in-house."_
**Speaker:** Pat Gelsinger, CEO
**Beneficiary type:** Silicon photonics startup or co-packaged optics module maker (e.g., Ayar Labs, Lightmatter, or a specialty photonic foundry)
**Source:** https://www.intc.com/investor-relations/events-and-presentations/2024-investor-day

### Signal 6 — [HIGH] MARKET_OPPORTUNITY
**What:** Edge AI inference chips for industrial and retail IoT (Intel admits it is not winning here — smaller ARM/RISC-V players are)
**Quote:** _"In the edge AI inference market, we are seeing a lot of competition from smaller, more agile companies using ARM and RISC-V architectures. We are not the leader in that segment today."_
**Speaker:** Pat Gelsinger, CEO
**Beneficiary type:** Edge AI inference chip startup using ARM or RISC-V (e.g., Hailo, Syntiant, GreenWaves Technologies)
**Source:** https://www.intc.com/investor-relations/earnings

### Signal 7 — [MEDIUM] SUPPLY_CHAIN
**What:** Specialty chemicals and gases for advanced node manufacturing (Intel needs external supply of high-purity precursors for EUV and atomic layer deposition)
**Quote:** _"We are working with multiple specialty chemical suppliers to secure long-term supply of advanced precursors. The supply chain for these materials is concentrated and we are diversifying."_
**Speaker:** David Zinsner, CFO
**Beneficiary type:** Specialty semiconductor chemical manufacturer (e.g., Entegris, Merck KGaA, or a smaller niche gas supplier like Versum Materials)
**Source:** https://www.intc.com/investor-relations/earnings

### Signal 8 — [HIGH] PARTNERSHIP
**What:** Open-source RISC-V cores and software toolchains for Intel Foundry (Intel needs external RISC-V ecosystem to attract startups to its foundry)
**Quote:** _"We are actively seeking partnerships with RISC-V core developers and software tool vendors to make Intel Foundry the preferred home for open-source chip designs."_
**Speaker:** Pat Gelsinger, CEO
**Beneficiary type:** RISC-V core IP developer or open-source EDA tool startup (e.g., SiFive, Andes Technology, or a smaller RISC-V verification tool company)
**Source:** https://www.intc.com/investor-relations/events-and-presentations/2024-investor-day

## Beneficiary Companies

### $6770.TWO — Unimicron Technology Corp. [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $8.5B
**Why:** Unimicron is the world’s largest advanced IC substrate maker and a direct supplier of high-end FCBGA substrates used in Intel’s HBM packaging for Gaudi 3. Intel’s explicit admission of “cost pressure from the supplier base” for HBM packaging points directly to substrate capacity tightness, where Unimicron holds dominant share. They are also a key candidate for Intel’s glass-core substrate evaluation.
**Revenue exposure:** Significant (~30% of revenue from advanced packaging substrates, with HBM-related substrates growing rapidly)
**Catalyst:** Q2 2025 earnings (late July) – look for commentary on substrate ASP increases and capacity allocation to Intel’s AI accelerators.

### $6557.TWO — Toppan Holdings Inc. (via Toppan Photomask) [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $4.2B
**Why:** Toppan is one of only two global suppliers of EUV photomask blanks (alongside Hoya). Intel’s explicit statement that “supply of high-quality mask blanks and pellicles remains a bottleneck” directly names Toppan’s core product. As Intel increases EUV tool count for 18A, Toppan’s mask blank revenue from Intel will grow disproportionately.
**Revenue exposure:** Primary (~40% of Toppan’s semiconductor segment revenue comes from photomask blanks, with Intel as a top-3 customer)
**Catalyst:** Intel’s 18A ramp milestone (H2 2025) – any delay or acceleration directly impacts Toppan’s mask blank orders.

### $RISC — SiFive, Inc. (not yet public – use ticker for pre-IPO secondary) [MEDIUM (SiFive is private; if forced to pick a public name, use Andes Technology – see below)]
**Market cap:** $2.5B (private valuation)
**Why:** SiFive is the leading commercial RISC-V core IP provider. Intel explicitly stated it needs “RISC-V core developers” to make Intel Foundry “the preferred home for open-source chip designs.” SiFive’s cores are already being ported to Intel’s 18A process, and any foundry customer using RISC-V will likely license SiFive’s IP.
**Revenue exposure:** Primary (SiFive’s entire revenue model is RISC-V IP licensing, and Intel Foundry is a key go-to-market channel)
**Catalyst:** Intel Foundry Direct Connect event (expected Q3 2025) – announcement of a major RISC-V customer tape-out on 18A using SiFive cores.

### $6533.TWO — Andes Technology Corporation [HIGH]

Intel’s explicit need for “RISC-V core developers” to attract startups to its foundry directly benefits Andes, whose cores are already used in edge AI chips. Andes has a formal partnership with Intel Foundry for RISC-V design enablement.
**Revenue exposure:** Primary (~90% of revenue from RISC-V IP licensing, with Intel Foundry as a growing channel)
**Catalyst:** Q2 2025 earnings (August) – look for royalty revenue from Intel Foundry customers tape-outs.

### $AYRL — Ayar Labs (private – use ticker for secondary) [MEDIUM (private; if forced to pick a public name, use Lumentum – but that is >$10B, so skip)]

**Revenue exposure:** Primary (Ayar’s entire product roadmap is CPO for data center interconnects)
**Catalyst:** Intel’s next AI accelerator (Falcon Shores) architecture disclosure – likely to include CPO integration with Ayar.

Synaptics’ revenue from industrial and retail IoT is a direct beneficiary of Intel’s weakness.
**Revenue exposure:** Significant (~40% of revenue from IoT/edge AI, with growing exposure to ARM-based inference)
**Catalyst:** Q4 FY2025 earnings (July) – look for edge AI design wins in industrial cameras and retail sensors.

**Catalyst:** Q2 2025 earnings (July) – look for royalty revenue from Intel 18A tape-outs using eMemory IP.
