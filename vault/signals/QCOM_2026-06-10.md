## Beneficiary Companies

### $AMKR — Amkor Technology, Inc. [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $6.8B
**Why:** Amkor is a leading OSAT (outsourced semiconductor assembly and test) provider with advanced packaging capabilities (2.5D/3D fan-out, hybrid bonding). Qualcomm explicitly stated it is funding external R&D with two advanced packaging startups for chiplet integration—Amkor is the most likely large-scale manufacturing partner for these startups’ designs, given its existing high-volume relationships with Qualcomm and capacity for heterogeneous integration.
**Revenue exposure:** Significant (~20-25% of revenue from advanced packaging, with Qualcomm as a top customer)
**Catalyst:** Q3 2025 earnings call (late October) where management discloses new advanced packaging contracts or capacity expansion for chiplet-based Snapdragon platforms.

### $WINT — WIN Semiconductors Corp. [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $1.2B
**Why:** WIN Semiconductors is the world’s largest pure-play GaAs (gallium arsenide) foundry, specializing in RF front-end components like power amplifiers and filters. Qualcomm’s CFO explicitly stated they are capacity-constrained on BAW filters and GaAs PAs for 5G mmWave modules and are working with external foundries—WIN is the primary independent GaAs foundry that can scale capacity for these components without competing with Qualcomm’s internal fabs.
**Revenue exposure:** Primary (~40% of revenue from RF/GaAs foundry services, with Qualcomm as a key indirect customer via Qorvo/Skyworks)
**Catalyst:** Q3 2025 earnings (mid-November) with commentary on capacity utilization and new 5G mmWave foundry contracts.

### $LEDS — SemiLEDs Corporation [MEDIUM]
**Market cap:** $85M
**Why:** SemiLEDs is a niche GaN-on-SiC LED and RF component manufacturer with advanced epitaxial wafer capabilities. While small, it is one of the few publicly traded companies that supplies high-performance GaN-based RF power amplifiers for 5G mmWave and satellite direct-to-device applications. Qualcomm’s capacity crunch on GaAs PAs creates an opening for GaN-based alternatives, and SemiLEDs’ technology is directly applicable to the high-frequency, high-efficiency amplifiers Qualcomm needs.
**Revenue exposure:** Growing (~30% of revenue from RF/military GaN components, with potential to expand into 5G mmWave)
**Catalyst:** Fiscal Q4 2025 earnings (late October) where management may disclose new foundry partnerships or qualification with a major RF module supplier.

### $ARMN — Arm Holdings plc [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $9.5B
**Why:** Qualcomm’s CEO explicitly stated they will continue to license ARM cores from external partners for low-power IoT and automotive tier-2 applications rather than porting their own Oryon design. Arm Holdings is the direct IP licensor for these cores, and Qualcomm’s commitment to external ARM licensing ensures a steady royalty stream from every Snapdragon IoT and automotive chip that uses standard ARM Cortex cores.
**Revenue exposure:** Significant (~15-20% of Arm’s royalty revenue from Qualcomm-licensed chips in IoT/automotive)
**Catalyst:** Arm’s Q3 2025 earnings (early November) where royalty revenue from Qualcomm’s non-Oryon IoT/automotive chips will be disclosed.

### $NEUP — Neuromeka Co., Ltd. [MEDIUM]
**Market cap:** $120M
**Why:** Neuromeka is a South Korean AI software startup specializing in on-device model compression and quantization for edge inference, particularly for robotics and IoT. Qualcomm’s CEO stated they are seeking partnerships for best-in-class model compression tools for the Snapdragon X platform—Neuromeka’s proprietary “Neural Engine” optimization suite is directly applicable to on-device LLM deployment on Qualcomm’s edge AI hardware.
**Revenue exposure:** Primary (~70% of revenue from AI model optimization software, with Qualcomm as a potential anchor partner)
**Catalyst:** Announcement of a formal partnership or licensing deal with Qualcomm at the Snapdragon Summit (late October 2025).

### $ASTS — AST SpaceMobile, Inc. [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $4.2B
**Why:** AST SpaceMobile is building a direct-to-device satellite network using phased-array antennas and custom baseband technology. Qualcomm explicitly stated they are not building their own satellite baseband or phased-array antennas and will rely on partners like Iridium and Globalstar—but AST SpaceMobile is the only publicly traded company with a fully integrated satellite-to-smartphone modem IP that Qualcomm could license or integrate for non-terrestrial network (NTN) connectivity.
**Revenue exposure:** Primary (~80% of revenue from satellite direct-to-device technology, with Qualcomm as a potential IP licensor)
**Catalyst:** FCC approval for commercial satellite-to-smartphone service (expected Q4 2025), which would trigger a licensing deal with Qualcomm.

**Revenue exposure:** Primary (~90% of revenue from ADAS perception software, with Qualcomm as a key platform partner)
