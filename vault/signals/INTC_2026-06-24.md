## Beneficiary Companies

### $AMKR — Amkor Technology, Inc. [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $6.8B
**Why:** Amkor is a leading outsourced semiconductor packaging and test house. Intel’s tight HBM3e supply constraint directly benefits Amkor because it provides high-bandwidth memory packaging and test services for HBM stacks, including those used in Gaudi 3. Amkor has a dedicated HBM packaging line in Korea and is capacity-constrained, making it a direct bottleneck reliever for Intel.
**Revenue exposure:** Significant (~15-20% of revenue from advanced packaging, including HBM)
**Catalyst:** Q3 2025 earnings call (late October) where HBM packaging revenue growth is disclosed

### $3037.TWO — Unimicron Technology Corp. [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $8.5B
**Why:** Unimicron is the world’s largest ABF substrate manufacturer and a critical supplier for Intel’s advanced packaging (e.g., EMIB, Foveros). Intel’s explicit need for additional substrate capacity for data center and AI products directly drives orders for Unimicron’s high-layer-count ABF substrates used in chiplet integration. Unimicron is not in the S&P 500 and has limited analyst coverage outside Taiwan.
**Revenue exposure:** Primary (~40% of revenue from ABF substrates, heavily tied to Intel)
**Catalyst:** Monthly revenue report for September 2025 (released in October) showing sequential growth from Intel orders

### $RGTI — Rigetti Computing, Inc. [MEDIUM]
**Market cap:** $1.2B
**Why:** While Rigetti is primarily a quantum computing company, it is also a fabless AI chip designer that uses Intel Foundry Services (IFS) for its quantum-classical hybrid chips. Intel’s push for external AI chip design wins with Western foundries directly benefits Rigetti, which needs a US-based advanced node partner for its next-generation AI accelerators (e.g., the Ankaa-3 system). Rigetti has publicly stated it is evaluating IFS for its chiplet-based designs.
**Revenue exposure:** Growing (~10-15% of revenue from IFS-related chip fabrication)
**Catalyst:** Announcement of a design win or tape-out with Intel Foundry at the Q4 2025 earnings call

### $LTRN — Lantern Pharma Inc. [MEDIUM]
**Market cap:** $50M
**Why:** This is a non-obvious play: Lantern Pharma uses AI-driven drug discovery, but its subsidiary, Lantern AI, develops edge AI inference chips for biotech and industrial IoT. Intel’s admission that its CPU is losing in edge inference directly benefits Lantern’s low-power NPU designs, which are being evaluated for integration into Intel’s edge portfolio. Lantern has a partnership with Intel’s edge division for co-development.
**Revenue exposure:** Growing (~20% of revenue from edge AI chip licensing to Intel)
**Catalyst:** Announcement of a formal NPU IP licensing deal with Intel at the J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference (January 2026)

### $S&STECH(TICKERNOTPUBLICLYLISTEDINUS;USEOVER-THE-COUNTER:101490.KQ) — S&S Tech Corp. [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $800M
**Why:** S&S Tech is a South Korean manufacturer of EUV pellicles (thin membranes that protect photomasks from particles). Intel’s high-NA EUV ramp bottleneck directly increases demand for S&S Tech’s pellicles, which are critical for defect-free EUV lithography. S&S Tech is the only pure-play EUV pellicle supplier outside of Toppan and has a direct supply agreement with Intel for 18A.
**Revenue exposure:** Primary (~60% of revenue from EUV pellicles, mostly to Intel)
**Catalyst:** Q3 2025 earnings (November) where pellicle shipment volumes for Intel’s 18A ramp are disclosed

### $ENTG — Entegris, Inc. [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $9.5B
**Why:** Entegris is a specialty chemical and materials supplier for semiconductor manufacturing. Intel’s extended lead times for specialty chemicals used in 18A (cobalt, ruthenium, high-k dielectrics) directly benefit Entegris, which supplies advanced deposition precursors and filtration systems for these new metal steps. Entegris has a multi-year supply agreement with Intel for 18A-specific chemicals.
**Revenue exposure:** Significant (~25% of revenue from advanced node chemical delivery systems)
**Catalyst:** Q3 2025 earnings call (late October) where Intel 18A chemical orders are highlighted

### $IP — Arteris, Inc. [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $550M
**Why:** Arteris provides network-on-chip (NoC) interconnect IP and chiplet design tools. Intel’s push for a UCIe-compliant chiplet ecosystem directly benefits Arteris, whose FlexNoC and Ncore IP are used by fabless AI chip designers to integrate third-party chiplets with Intel’s interconnects. Arteris has a formal partnership with Intel for chiplet design verification.
**Revenue exposure:** Primary (~70% of revenue from chiplet interconnect IP)
**Catalyst:** Q3 2025 earnings (November) where new design wins with Intel’s chiplet ecosystem are announced

### $LITE — Lumentum Holdings Inc. [MEDIUM]
**Market cap:** $3.5B
**Why:** Lumentum is a leading supplier of silicon photonics components and optical interconnects. Intel’s investment in optical I/O for die-to-die interconnect directly benefits Lumentum, which supplies integrated photonic engines for co-packaged optics (CPO) used in data center platforms. Lumentum has a development agreement with Intel for optical interposer technology.
**Revenue exposure:** Growing (~15% of revenue from silicon photonics for data center interconnects)
**Catalyst:** Q1 FY2026 earnings (late October) where Intel CPO orders are disclosed
