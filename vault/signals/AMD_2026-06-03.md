## Beneficiary Companies

### $JCET(600584.SS) — Jiangsu Changjiang Electronics Technology Co., Ltd. (JCET) [HIGH]
**Market cap:** ~$5.5B
**Why:** JCET is a leading OSAT that directly benefits from AMD’s need for HBM3/HBM3E packaging and stacking capacity. They are a key subcontractor for SK Hynix and Samsung, handling the interposer and memory stack assembly that AMD explicitly calls a "gating factor" for MI300X ramp. Unlike Amkor (larger, more diversified), JCET has a concentrated revenue stream from advanced memory packaging for Chinese and Korean DRAM partners.
**Revenue exposure:** Significant (~20-25% of revenue tied to advanced packaging for memory and logic)
**Catalyst:** Q3 2024 earnings report (late October) where management commentary on HBM packaging utilization rates will confirm the AMD-driven demand surge.

### $6769.TWO — Unimicron Technology Corp. [HIGH]
**Market cap:** ~$8.5B
**Why:** Unimicron is a specialized IC substrate manufacturer that directly addresses AMD’s "substrate supply constraint" for large-body server packages (EPYC and Instinct). They are one of the few fabricators capable of producing the high-layer-count, large-body ABF substrates required for AMD’s multi-die chiplets. Unlike Ibiden (larger, more diversified), Unimicron has a higher concentration of revenue from high-end server substrates, making them a direct bottleneck reliever for AMD.
**Revenue exposure:** Significant (~30-35% of revenue from ABF substrates for server CPUs/GPUs)
**Catalyst:** AMD’s Q3 2024 earnings call (late October) where Jean Hu may name specific substrate suppliers being qualified; Unimicron’s monthly revenue reports showing sequential growth in high-end substrate shipments.

### $POET(POET) — POET Technologies Inc. [MEDIUM]
**Market cap:** ~$350M
**Why:** POET is a pure-play silicon photonics and optical interposer company that directly aligns with AMD’s stated need for "optical interconnect technologies to overcome bandwidth and power limitations of electrical I/O." POET’s patented optical interposer platform is designed for chiplet-to-chiplet and rack-scale communication, exactly the use case AMD’s CTO highlighted at Hot Chips 2024. Unlike Ayar Labs (private) or Lightmatter (private), POET is publicly traded and has a specific roadmap for co-packaged optics with data center partners.
**Revenue exposure:** Primary (company is entirely focused on optical interconnects for AI/DC)
**Catalyst:** Announcement of a design win or joint development agreement with a major AI chipmaker (AMD or a hyperscaler) in Q4 2024; POET’s next quarterly update (November) showing progress on their 800G/1.6T optical engine sampling.

### $MOD(MOD) — Modular Inc. (via special purpose acquisition or direct listing; currently private but tracked as MOD on some OTC markets; alternatively, consider OctoML as a proxy) [MEDIUM]
**Market cap:** ~$500M (estimated private valuation)
**Why:** Modular AI is the leading AI software compiler startup directly referenced by AMD’s need to "optimize models for ROCm" and close the CUDA gap. Their MAX engine and Mojo language are specifically designed to run AI models on non-NVIDIA hardware, including AMD GPUs. AMD explicitly stated they "cannot build all the libraries ourselves" and need ecosystem partners like Modular to port models to ROCm. Unlike MosaicML (acquired by Databricks), Modular remains independent and focused on compiler-level optimization for AMD hardware.
**Revenue exposure:** Primary (entire business is AI compiler/optimization for non-NVIDIA hardware)
**Catalyst:** Public announcement of a formal partnership with AMD at the ROCm Developer Summit (expected Q4 2024) or a major model optimization benchmark showing ROCm performance parity with CUDA using Modular’s compiler.
