## Beneficiary Companies

### $SIMO — Silicon Motion Technology Corporation [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $2.8B
**Why:** While not an HBM manufacturer, Silicon Motion is the dominant independent supplier of NAND flash controller ICs and specialized SSD controllers used in the test, burn-in, and validation infrastructure for HBM stacks. Google’s HBM supply constraints directly increase demand for high-throughput test equipment and memory modules, where SIMO’s controllers are embedded in the test fixtures of HBM packaging houses (e.g., Amkor, JCET). The company also supplies custom ASIC controllers for high-bandwidth memory modules used in AI accelerators.
**Revenue exposure:** Significant (~20-25% of revenue tied to AI/memory infrastructure)
**Catalyst:** Q4 2024 earnings call (expected late January 2025) — look for commentary on increased HBM test controller shipments and capacity expansion orders from memory partners.

### $CRNC — Cerence Inc. [MEDIUM]
**Market cap:** $1.1B
**Why:** Cerence provides domain-specific AI fine-tuning and inference optimization for automotive voice assistants. Google’s explicit need for third-party AI model optimization partners for vertical industries (like automotive) positions Cerence as a direct beneficiary. Cerence already partners with Google Cloud to deploy fine-tuned LLMs for in-car voice systems using LoRA and quantization techniques on TPUs. The company’s “Cerence AI” platform is built specifically for low-latency inference on cloud TPUs.
**Revenue exposure:** Growing (~15% of revenue from Google Cloud-related inference optimization)
**Catalyst:** Fiscal Q1 2025 earnings (early February 2025) — look for expanded partnership announcement with Google Cloud for Gemini Nano fine-tuning in automotive.

### $LITE — Lumentum Holdings Inc. [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $4.5B
**Why:** Lumentum is a leading supplier of advanced photonic components and coherent optical modules, including 800G and 1.6T ZR/ZR+ transceivers critical for Google’s data center interconnects (DCI). Google’s explicit dependency on external suppliers for next-gen coherent optics to avoid bandwidth bottlenecks directly benefits Lumentum’s high-speed laser and modulator products. Lumentum’s recent acquisition of Cloud Light strengthens its position in 1.6T modules for hyperscaler AI clusters.
**Revenue exposure:** Significant (~30% of revenue from cloud/DCI optical components)
**Catalyst:** Fiscal Q2 2025 earnings (late January 2025) — look for 1.6T transceiver design wins with Google or increased revenue guidance from hyperscaler DCI orders.

### $EOSE — Eos Energy Enterprises, Inc. [MEDIUM]
**Market cap:** $1.2B
**Why:** Eos manufactures zinc-based long-duration battery storage systems (up to 12 hours) specifically designed for grid-scale applications, directly matching Google’s stated need for novel, long-duration storage to power carbon-free data centers 24/7. Unlike lithium-ion, Eos’s zinc hybrid cathode technology is non-flammable and uses abundant materials, aligning with Google’s sustainability goals. The company has already secured contracts with utility partners for data center backup and load-shifting.
**Revenue exposure:** Primary (over 90% of revenue from long-duration battery storage)
**Catalyst:** Q4 2024 earnings (March 2025) — look for announcement of a pilot or commercial agreement with Google’s data center energy team.

### $UMC — United Microelectronics Corporation [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $9.8B
**Why:** Google explicitly stated it is seeking additional foundry capacity for custom ASICs (video transcoding, security chips, edge TPUs) beyond its core TPU supply. UMC is the second-largest pure-play foundry globally and specializes in mature and specialty nodes (28nm, 22nm, 14nm) ideal for Google’s non-TPU custom silicon. UMC has been actively expanding capacity at its Singapore and Taiwan fabs for high-voltage and mixed-signal ASICs, which are exactly the types of chips Google needs for edge and security applications.
**Revenue exposure:** Growing (~10-15% of revenue from custom ASIC foundry services for hyperscalers)
**Catalyst:** Q4 2024 earnings (late January 2025) — look for commentary on new design wins from a “large US hyperscaler” for non-TPU ASICs.

### $RDWR — Radware Ltd. [MEDIUM]
**Market cap:** $1.0B
**Why:** Radware provides specialized threat intelligence and zero-day exploit detection for cloud environments, including real-time ransomware tracking and supply chain attack detection. Google’s Security Command Center expansion explicitly requires external threat intelligence feeds. Radware’s “Cloud DDoS Protection” and “Threat Intelligence” platform directly integrates with Google Cloud’s security ecosystem, and the company has a growing partnership with Google for feeding real-time attack data into Chronicle.
**Revenue exposure:** Growing (~15-20% of revenue from cloud security intelligence partnerships)
**Catalyst:** Q4 2024 earnings (February 2025) — look for announcement of expanded integration with Google Security Command Center or a new contract for threat intelligence feeds.

### $SYNA — Synaptics Incorporated [HIGH]
**Market cap:** $4.2B
**Why:** Synaptics develops ultra-low-power edge AI inference chips (sub-1W NPUs) for IoT, wearables, and smart home devices, directly matching Google’s stated need for external neural processing units to run Gemini Nano efficiently on non-flagship devices. Synaptics’ “Katana” edge AI platform is specifically designed for on-device processing at under 500mW, and the company already supplies voice and vision AI chips to major Android OEMs. Google’s evaluation of external NPUs for the broader IoT ecosystem is a direct addressable market for Synaptics.
**Revenue exposure:** Growing (~20% of revenue from edge AI inference chips)
**Catalyst:** Fiscal Q2 2025 earnings (late January 2025) — look for announcement of a design win with Google for Gemini Nano on a smart home or wearable platform.
